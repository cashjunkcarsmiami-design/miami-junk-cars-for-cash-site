Requesting an Offer for a Car With Water in the Trunk in North Miami

By Miami Junk Cars For Cash · October 5, 2026

Water in a trunk can come with odors, stained trim or other damage that is not obvious in an exterior photograph. A North Miami seller should describe what was observed without guessing at the source or treating the vehicle as undamaged.

Report observations instead of a diagnosis

Explain where water was found, when you noticed it and whether the vehicle has been used since. Mention damp carpets, visible staining or known warning messages if present. Do not label the problem a harmless seal leak or a flooded vehicle unless you have reliable information supporting that description. Tell the buyer what has been inspected and what remains unknown so the evaluation uses the actual condition.

Photograph accessible damage

Show the trunk area and any visible interior effects without removing heavy trim or opening inaccessible compartments. Add exterior views and the car location. Say whether the photos were taken before or after cleaning, because a dry photograph may not reveal the earlier condition. If belongings remain in a compartment, explain that before handoff and arrange to remove accessible personal items before collection.

Include movement and document details

State whether the car currently starts or rolls, whether keys are available and which ownership documents you have. Do not promise normal operation based on a previous trip. Confirm collection requirements and whether the offer reflects the water issue described. Update the buyer if more damage is discovered between the quote and pickup so an earlier message is not treated as the final description.

Should I say the car was flooded if I only found water in the trunk?

Describe the water and damage you observed. Explain that the source and extent are unknown rather than giving an unverified diagnosis.
